Nigerian Military Counter-Insurgency Operating Environment

Reporting period: 24 – 31 August 2026 (7 days) | Issued: Monday 31 August 2026. The North East ground pause ended: ISWAP attacked 101 Special Forces Battalion at Kadiya (Kukawa) on the night of 28–29 August and, among the material recovered afterwards, troops found TWO ANTI-AIRCRAFT ROUNDS — the first physical AAA-signature datapoint in this brief's tracking window. The air campaign meanwhile shifted toward logistics, striking a 44-boat concentration at Kangarwa. Outside the theatre, a mutiny in Niamey on 29 August briefly took the military airbase at Diori Hamani. Background from the baseline assessment and earlier issues is not repeated.

1. Bottom Line Up Front

  • The base-attack pause ended at Kadiya, Kukawa — and the exploitation recovered two anti-aircraft rounds. This is the week's single most air-relevant item. At about 22:45 on Friday 28 August, ISWAP attacked troops of 101 Special Forces Battalion (Sector 3 OPHK) near Kadiya village, Kukawa LGA. Ground troops with OPHK air support repelled the assault; the NAF then flew two precision strikes on the withdrawal — the first against ~10 fighters sheltering under trees (all assessed killed), the second against fighters fleeing toward the Tunbuns/Kangarwa axis, coordinated with MNJTF ISR assets (~15 assessed killed, 10 motorcycles destroyed). Exploitation along the withdrawal route at 06:00 on 29 August found one further body: 26 claimed in total. Friendly cost: four soldiers wounded (stable) and one MRAP damaged. Recovered items included 253 rounds of 7.62x54mm PKT, 12 rounds of 7.62mm special — and two anti-aircraft rounds. Two rounds is ammunition, not a system, and the likeliest reading is heavy-machine-gun ammunition carried for ground use. But it is the first AAA-calibre material this brief has seen reported recovered, it sits on the Lake Chad axis where our strike tempo is concentrated, and it should be treated as an indicator to confirm, not a curiosity. Single-chain (Zagazola/military sources).
  • The air campaign moved from personnel to logistics: 44 boats sighted at Kangarwa, 12 destroyed. At 12:55 on 25 August an ISR mission detected 44 ISWAP logistics boats drawn up on the riverbank near Kangarwa, Kukawa LGA, at coordinates given as ~13.266°N 13.657°E. NAF aircraft identified in the report as 'Phantoms' struck first; an armed-reconnaissance platform remained overhead, observed fighters attempting to salvage and load damaged hulls, and re-attacked the cluster. BDA: four killed, 12 boats destroyed. Two things matter operationally. First, 32 hulls were left — this axis will be re-tasked, and the remainder will disperse and move at night. Second, the re-attack-on-salvagers technique is now a repeating pattern, three weeks running: a struck area is not a cold area. Note also that Kadiya is on the same Kukawa axis, three days later.
  • Adamawa re-opened: a Boko Haram camp complex destroyed on Kopre Mountain, 21 claimed killed. Between 01:30 on 26 August and 04:10 on 27 August, Army troops with a tactical team moved by stealth onto a camp in the Kopre Mountain general area of Hong LGA and assaulted it. Reported result: 21 Boko Haram killed, others escaping wounded, the camp and its logistics destroyed along with motorcycles, bicycles and jerrycans of fuel; AK-47s, magazines and 7.62mm special ammunition recovered. One member of the supporting hunter component was killed; no military equipment losses. This is the first substantial Adamawa camp complex reported inside the rolling window and suggests the Hong / Mandara-fringe axis is re-forming as a JAS rear area. Adamawa goes 2→3 on the heatmap.
  • REGIONAL — a mutiny in Niamey on 29 August struck the military airbase at Diori Hamani, the AES counter-terrorism and drone hub. Multi-source. Mutinous soldiers attacked the airbase (co-located with Niamey's civilian airport and home to military aircraft, drones and an Alliance of Sahel States counter-terrorism unit), then the state broadcaster and the presidency. The regime and its Russian Africa Corps partners retook the base late on Saturday; Niamey was reported normal by Sunday and the government says the mutiny is contained. For an air unit this is an airspace and coordination item, not just politics: Niger's southern border is the Lakurawa/ISSP seam, Nigeria–Niger security cooperation has been poor since 2023 and is now worse, and Nigerien airspace management should be treated as unpredictable for the next several weeks. Watch for opportunistic activity on the border while Niamey is looking inward.
  • Force protection otherwise holds, but base attacks ran in BOTH theatres this week, and CASEVAC infrastructure improved. No enemy drone use, no MANPADS employment, no GPS or EW interference and no aircraft or UAV losses — thirteen weeks, verified again by search this week. But alongside Kadiya, a large force attacked FOB Sifawa barracks and two communities in Bodinga LGA, Sokoto, in daylight on 26 August: repelled, one soldier killed in action, an APC and a Buffalo transport damaged. On the positive side, the COAS commissioned a helipad at 44 Nigerian Army Reference Hospital, Kaduna on 25 August, explicitly to speed air evacuation of wounded soldiers, alongside a new critical care centre — a receiving facility worth reconnoitring before you need it.

2. North East Developments

Window note: a busier week than the last, on the ground and in the air. The base-attack pause that had held since Mandaragirau (15 August) ended at Kadiya on 28–29 August; a village attack at Juhuhi (Hawul) opened the window on 23 August; ISWAP also attacked 212 Battalion at Tungushe in Jere LGA. The air component worked logistics as hard as personnel. The factional war inside the enclaves is now corroborated by official reporting rather than resting on a single chain.

Kadiya, Kukawa — the base attack and the AAA find, 28–29 August

Detail as at Section 1. The sequencing is worth noting for tasking: human intelligence on terrorist movement around Kadiya preceded the attack; the ground repel was air-supported in contact; two follow-on precision strikes worked the withdrawal, the second explicitly coordinated with MNJTF ISR; a ground exploitation team walked the withdrawal route at first light. That is a complete find-fix-finish-exploit cycle in under eight hours, and it is the exploitation phase that produced the anti-aircraft rounds. If your unit can get sight of the actual calibre through the ground chain — 12.7mm and 14.5mm imply a heavy MG or ZPU role; 23mm would imply something materially worse — that single data point is worth chasing.

Kangarwa boat strike and the logistics campaign, 25 August

Detail as at Section 1. Lake Chad waterways move personnel, weapons, food and fuel between ISWAP locations, so a 44-boat concentration is a high-value, fixed, observable target set — and one that regenerates. Related counter-logistics activity in the window: troops of 159 Battalion arrested three suspected ISWAP logistics suppliers during a curfew operation in Geidam, Yobe, who had allegedly been buying petroleum products and other supplies for fighters on the Gorom–Damasak axis (₦185,950, two phones and an ID card recovered); 11 suspects were picked up in Monguno LGA (eight collaborators, two couriers or spies, one fighter); and a suspected ISWAP operative was arrested at Mairari, Guzamala LGA with an image of armed men on him.

Continuing strike tempo and contacts

  • Sambisa precision strike — reported 30/31 August. The OPHK air component struck a terrorist enclave in the Sambisa axis after sighting three fighters entering a structure and a fourth parking a motorcycle nearby; the strike destroyed the building and the motorcycle and killed four (official OPHK operational report via NAN/P.M. News). Sambisa coverage is therefore continuous alongside the Lake Chad work.
  • Infiltration and contact. Troops of the 81 Division Task Force Battalion under OPHK foiled an ISWAP infiltration attempt and killed four (reported 27 August). ISWAP attacked 212 Battalion at Tungushe, Jere LGA — repelled. 115 Task Force Battalion with the CJTF made contact with ISWAP at Rumirigo, Askira-Uba LGA; the fighters fled leaving a pistol and seven rounds of 9mm.
  • MSR IEDs continue. Troops discovered a detonated IED on the Dikwa–Marte road on about 25 August following a reported explosion on the route. That keeps the Dikwa/Marte/Ajiri road complex as the standing IED belt — the fifth device or device area reported there inside a fortnight. Bridge and junction approaches remain the predictable emplacement points.

Factional war now corroborated in official reporting

A suspected female ISWAP member surrendered with her two children at Mafa; the official OPHK report states she fled the Burari enclave with them 'following violent clashes between rival terrorist factions.' Until now the ISWAP–JAS factional fighting thread has rested almost entirely on Zagazola security-source reporting. An official operational report attributing a surrender to factional violence is the first independent-of-that-chain corroboration this brief has recorded. Two internally displaced minors from Chikide in the Mandara Mountains were also intercepted on the Wala–Gwoza–Pulka road on 29 August. Troops conducted wet-season clearance patrols across Yobe communities through the window.

Schools thread — roughly day 108 and day 63, still unresolved

No release, negotiation or proof-of-life reporting surfaced in this window for the 42 Mussa pupils (taken 15 May) or the 36 Lassa students and one staff member (taken 29 June) — 78 children and one teacher in total. Senator Ali Ndume publicly pressed the Army to redouble efforts on 22 August, following the Uba parents' protest reported last week. Nothing has changed since.

3. Air / ISR & Force-Protection Watch Items

  • Treat the Kadiya anti-aircraft rounds as an indicator to confirm, not an alarm — and not a nothing. Two rounds recovered on a battlefield is weak evidence of an air-defence capability and reasonable evidence that AAA-calibre ammunition is circulating on the Lake Chad axis. Revise the standing assumption from 'no AAA signature at all' to 'no AAA employment observed; AAA-calibre ammunition present in the Kukawa area.' Practical actions: seek the calibre through the ground chain; brief crews transiting the Kukawa / Kangarwa / Tunbuns axis on it; and treat any subsequent recovery, or the first report of tracer or airburst, as a priority report rather than a routine one.
  • Logistics-boat interdiction is now a recurring ISR task with a specific signature. The Kangarwa engagement began as an ISR sighting of hulls drawn up at a fixed riverbank point and ended with a fighting aircraft re-attacking salvagers. Expect repeat tasking on the same axis, dispersal and night movement of the surviving 32 hulls, and value in riverbank pattern-of-life rather than one-off searches. Also note the deconfliction implication: the Kukawa follow-on strike was coordinated with MNJTF ISR — coalition assets, not only NAF-internal ones, are cueing over the Lake Chad approaches.
  • Multi-axis, multi-strike work continues; assume struck areas are re-attacked. Inside eight days the air component worked Kangarwa (25 Aug), Sambisa (30 Aug) and Kadiya's withdrawal route in two separate strikes (28–29 Aug). Verify ROZ activation, altitude blocks and the strike-clearance chain over the Kukawa/Lake Chad approaches, the Sambisa enclave belt and the Biu/Hawul axis before every launch, and do not assume a target area is cold minutes after a strike.
  • Regional airspace: the Niamey mutiny degrades an already poor picture on the northern border. Diori Hamani hosts Nigerien military aircraft and drones, an AES counter-terrorism unit and Russian Africa Corps personnel, and was contested for the better part of a day on 29 August. Even with the base retaken, assume unpredictable Nigerien airspace management, possible short-notice closures or NOTAM activity, and heightened border sensitivity for several weeks. Nigeria–Niger intelligence sharing has been blocked since the 2023 coup — this makes any cross-border deconfliction harder, not easier, precisely while Lakurawa sits on that seam.
  • CASEVAC: 44 NARH Kaduna now has a commissioned helipad. The COAS commissioned the pad on 25 August at the 44 Nigerian Army Reference Hospital, Kaduna, explicitly to speed emergency evacuation of wounded soldiers, alongside the new Waidi Shaibu Critical Care Centre at the same site. If you fly casualties out of the North West, this materially changes the receiving picture. Confirm pad dimensions, approach and departure lanes, lighting and the control arrangement before you need them rather than on the night.
  • Base attacks are running in both theatres in the same week — and the North West one was in daylight. Sifawa (Bodinga LGA, Sokoto) was hit at 09:20 on 26 August by a large force attacking the barracks and the Badau and Danchadi communities simultaneously. Troops of 28 Special Forces Brigade from FOB Sifawa with FOB Shagari, the 8 Division Strike Team and a Mobile Strike Team repelled it; one soldier was killed, an APC lost two tyres and a Buffalo transport had its fuel tank and mirrors destroyed. Daylight, multi-pronged, against a barracks: if tasked into the North West, do not assume the threat picture there is materially softer than the North East's.
  • Threat systems: nothing employed — thirteen weeks. No enemy drone use, no MANPADS, no GPS or EW interference, no aircraft or UAV losses. The last documented armed-drone use against a Nigerian base remains Sabon Gari, Damboa (late January 2026); the last confirmed airframe loss remains the 2 January CH-4 (technical failure, Kontagora). Searches this week returned no new Nigerian air loss. The standing caution stands and the Kadiya AAA find sharpens it: ISWAP's incentive to answer strike losses with unmanned systems or with air defence is rising alongside our tempo.
  • US dimension — no new activity; posture assumption unchanged. No new AFRICOM announcements concerning Nigeria this window. The standing picture is the December 2025 Bauni Forest strikes against Lakurawa in Sokoto and the May 2026 coordinated strikes against ISIS in the North East; ISS assesses that sporadic strikes alone have not degraded Lakurawa, which is now operating from Borgu. US kinetic activity remains dormant, US ISR presence remains possible — keep it in the deconfliction picture.
  • Information integrity: no fabricated-loss campaign this week, but the fabrication tempo continues. PRNigeria debunked a viral video falsely portraying visitors to a Nigerian senator as bandit kingpins (29 August). Together with last week's AI-generated obituaries targeting the COAS and the earlier DHQ account-breach warning, the rule is unchanged: treat any loss, shoot-down or casualty claim involving air assets as unverified until confirmed through the chain.

4. Other Theatres in Brief

  • North West — a barracks attack, and IEDs that are now killing civilians. Beyond Sifawa (Section 3): Sector 2 Op FANSAN YAMMA troops killed several terrorists and recovered arms, motorcycles and rustled livestock in Rabah LGA, Sokoto (27–28 August). In Zamfara an IED on the Gurusu–Gwashi road in Bukkuyum LGA killed two civilians at 08:04 on 26 August, and a second device on the same road was recovered and safely destroyed the following day — North West IED emplacement has moved from targeting troops to killing rural road users, and Zagazola ran a standalone analysis on the widening civilian IED threat. Also: two suspected terrorists arrested with an AK-47, ammunition and communication equipment at FOB Kaura Namoda (25 August); Fansan Yamma arrested three collaborators and destroyed enclaves in Kaduna and Katsina (29 August); a ransom exchange was foiled and a kidnapped woman rescued in Danko-Wasagu, Kebbi; 1 Division troops recovered 97 rustled livestock in Gwarzo, Kano; and bandit leader Kachalla Namadi was killed by rival gunmen in Zamfara. The NFIU reported terrorist financiers moving funds through women's bank accounts and crowdfunding (25 August).
  • North Central / Middle Belt — very high volume, comparatively low lethality; the reciprocal cycle is now largely about cattle and farmland. Plateau produced a stream of OPEP items in both directions. Against communities: a woman killed in an ambush at Batok 2, Riyom (28 August); a body recovered at Bachi, Fang District, Riyom; three killed at Gwarim near Riyom while an attack on Rim was foiled (26–27 August); a motorcyclist killed on the Matelem–Ndung road, Tangur District, Bokkos; two passengers abducted after gunmen blocked the Bokkos–Barkin Ladi road (28 August); a man abducted at Angwan Kaji, Bassa. Against herders: Berom militia killed 21 cows in a targeted attack in Riyom, and two further rustling attempts were foiled with 19 and five cattle recovered. Troops also foiled armed Fulani attacks at Manja Village and Manja Forest in Mangu, foiled a kidnapping in Jos North at 03:45 rescuing two, and arrested herders over farmland destruction and militia over a cattle attack at Pankshin. The GOC 3 Division and Comd OPEP engaged Irigwe farmers and stakeholders in Bassa over attacks and farm destruction. In Benue, Operation Zenda drew public criticism after a clearance operation around Ayilamo, Logo LGA reportedly killed some 200 cattle — a civil-military friction item to watch. In Niger State, Op SAVANNAH SHIELD foiled a kidnapping of farmers at Tudun Harara, Mariga LGA (26 August). In Kaduna's Jema'a LGA a farmer–herder clash at Rafin Dadi killed one, and troops averted a protest over farm destruction at Fido.
  • South East — quiet after the August flare. No new IPOB/ESN camp raids or IED incidents were reported in this window following the 15 and 22 August Anambra operations. Anambra holds 3 on the heatmap; with the November governorship approaching, expect tempo to rise again rather than settle.
  • Niger Delta / Gulf of Guinea — twelfth consecutive quiet week for militancy; enforcement heavy. No new pipeline or maritime attack claims. 6 Division troops found refining equipment and 3,120 litres of stolen crude in the Okarki Forest, Ahoada West LGA (Rivers); 343 Artillery Regiment intercepted tugboats and barges carrying 21,000 bags of illegally mined ilmenite 'black sand' in Bayelsa, and Op DELTA SANITY intercepted a further 10,400 bags with five arrests; two suspects were arrested over illegal petroleum products in Rivers.
  • Regional and political weather — Niamey dominates; JNIM keeps the Sahel pressure on. Beyond the mutiny (Section 1): JNIM ambushed Malian Armed Forces and Russian Africa Corps personnel near Erintedjeft in the Tombouctou region on about 28 August, killing at least 19 — the Sahel dynamic that feeds Lakurawa reinforcement into the Nigeria–Niger–Benin tri-border. Domestically, the DSS instructed counsel to seek adjournment of its cyber-bullying case against Omoyele Sowore until after the 2027 presidential election (30 August), and the Inspector-General of Police ordered a nationwide crackdown on mob killings (24 August) after a run of jungle-justice incidents. No gubernatorial changes this week.
